Business strategy

Competitive Positioning Map

When you need to see where your offer stands against alternatives before choosing a direction.

You are a strategy consultant. I run [your business] selling [your product] to [target customer]. My main competitors are [competitor 1], [competitor 2] and [competitor 3].
Compare us on the five factors buyers care about most in [your industry], and pick those factors yourself. Be candid about where we are weaker.
Constraints: base claims only on what I tell you and on widely known facts, and flag any assumption.
Format: a table of factors versus players with a 1-5 score, then three white-space opportunities and one recommended positioning in two sentences.
Business strategy

SWOT With Actions

When a standard SWOT feels too generic and you want concrete next moves from it.

Act as a business analyst. Build a SWOT analysis for [your business], which [one-sentence description] in [market/region]. Key facts: [revenue stage, team size, main channels, biggest recent change].
Constraints: every point must be specific to this business, not generic; limit each quadrant to four items; rank items by impact.
Format: the four quadrants as bullet lists, then a section called 'Moves' that pairs strengths with opportunities and weaknesses with threats, giving one action each, an owner role, and a 30-day success measure.
Business strategy

Pricing Strategy Review

When you are setting or revisiting prices and want options with trade-offs, not just one number.

You are a pricing strategist. My product is [your product], currently priced at [current price] for [target customer]. My costs per unit or per customer are about [cost], and alternatives in the market cost [competitor price range].
Task: propose three pricing models (for example tiered, usage-based, flat) and evaluate each against margin, simplicity and fit with how customers buy.
Constraints: state assumptions, avoid manipulative tactics such as fake discounts.
Format: a comparison table, your recommended model, a suggested price test with duration and the metric that decides the winner.

Pre-Mortem Risk Analysis

When you are about to commit to a plan and want to find how it could fail first.

Act as a skeptical advisor running a pre-mortem. Imagine that on [date, e.g. 12 months from now] my initiative [describe the plan, budget and timeline] has clearly failed.
Task: list the ten most plausible reasons for failure, covering market, execution, people, money and timing. For each, give a likelihood (low/medium/high), an early warning sign I could observe, and a cheap mitigation.
Constraints: be blunt, avoid generic risks like 'poor communication' unless tied to specifics.
Format: a table sorted by likelihood times impact, then the three risks I should address this week.

Unit Economics Check

When you want to know if your business model makes money per customer before you scale spend.

You are a finance-minded operator. Review the unit economics for [your business]. Inputs: average price [price], gross margin [percent or costs], customer acquisition cost [CAC], average customer lifetime or retention [months or rate], and monthly fixed costs [amount].
Task: calculate contribution margin, payback period and lifetime value to CAC ratio, and show the formulas.
Constraints: if an input is missing, state a reasonable placeholder and mark it clearly.
Format: a short calculation table, a plain-English verdict, and the two levers that would improve the numbers most with example changes and results.

Positioning and Messaging Pillars

When your marketing sounds generic and you need a clear core message to build everything on.

You are a brand strategist. Product: [your product]. Audience: [target audience]. Their main problem: [problem]. Alternatives they use now: [alternatives]. Our real differentiator: [what is truly different and provable].
